\begin{abstract}
    This paper investigates the evolution of ``loyalty signaling'' by Hong Kong Chief Executives (CEs) toward the central government. While the definition of ``loyalty'' 
    remains conceptually fluid and vague in Chinese politics, we propose a six-dimensional framework to categorize how political alignment is communicated. And the authors 
    measure the frequency of loyalty signaling by CEs through two proxies: (i) \textit{loyalty-signaling keyword density} in CE speeches and press releases, and (ii) the 
    semantic similarity between the speeches of CEs and central leaders. The results reveal a distinct cyclical pattern in CE's loyalty-signaling discourse which intensifies 
    following periods of socio-political friction or ``trust crises'' between Hong Kong and the Central Government, yet with no evidence suggesting a wholesale importation of 
    Mainland-style rhetorical system into Hong Kong. It is thus suggested that loyalty signaling is employed by CEs as a reparative strategy aiming at signaling political alignment 
    to Beijing and regaining its political trust during times of uncertainty and turmoil, so that Hong Kong might gradually recover more room for high autonomy and perhaps get 
    back on track towards democratization as prescribed in the Basic Law. 

    
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

    \rhostart{S}ince the implementation of the Law of the People's Republic of China on Safeguarding National Security in the Hong Kong Special Administrative Region (`NSL') 
    in 2020 and the subsequent top-down overhaul of Hong Kong's electoral system which produces members of the Legislative Council (`LegCo') and the Chief Executive of the HKSAR 
    government (`CE') by Beijing, many people have observed a sharp increase of the references to the PRC central authorities, their remarks, their ideology and their policy directions
     in the SAR government's public discourse. This phenomenon appears to be increasingly pronounced after the incumbent CE, John Lee, took office in mid-2022. John Lee is viewed 
     by many as proactively engaging the SAR government in a process of ``Mainlandization'' for the purpose of signaling loyalty to the Central People's Government (`CPG') \parencite{chan2023ccp}, 
     as opposed to his predecessors, who did not adopt such a ``sycophantic'' gesture in signaling loyalty to Beijing. Focusing on the discursive aspect of the CE's 
    loyalty-signaling behaviour, our research aims to explore the extent to which the CEs' public addresses resonate with the language of the CPG and then explore the underlying 
    logic behind their loyalty-signaling behaviours.

    This paper will firstly lay out the general theoretical framework on loyalty signaling by lower-level officials to the central authority and the important role of language 
    and discourse amid the loyalty-signaling process by reviewing the existing literature. On this basis, we will conceptualize the phenomenon of ``loyalty signaling through 
    language'' in the political context of Hong Kong, according to which six types of keywords used to communicate loyalty will be proposed (`\textit{Loyalty Signaling Keywords}'). 
    We will then quantify the extent to which different CEs signaled loyalty through discourse by measuring the density of loyalty keywords in CEs' speeches and the CE office's 
    press releases, with the semantic similarities between the public addresses of CEs and central leaders as a further reference that enriches our analysis.
	
    
\section{General theoretical framework of Loyalty Signaling}

    \textcite{qian2024loyalty} give three fundamental propositions regarding lower-level government officials' loyalty signaling behaviours towards the central authority in an 
    authoritarian regime: i) officials with a less-trusted background have a greater incentive to signal their loyalty to the authoritarian leader; ii) credible loyalty signaling 
    becomes particularly significant for an official if his or her career prospect and political survival is largely determined by the ruler's favor, which is often the case in an 
    authoritarian regime; iii) one of the most effective ways for a less-trusted official to express his or her loyalty is to engage in ``unpopular and costly'' tasks on the ruler's 
    behalf and for the ruler's benefit. Whilst \textcite{qian2024loyalty} focus more on how local bureaucrats used political persecution to express loyalty amid the Anti-Rightist 
    Campaign in the early years of the PRC, \textcite{baturo2025personalization} highlight the local officials' politicized framing of their public discourse as a means of loyalty signaling 
    in an authoritarian regime. They find that the state governors in Russia signaled their loyalty to Putin by making more references to him and his political agenda in the 
    addresses to their regional legislatures \parencite{baturo2025personalization}. 

    \subsection{Loyalty-signaling paradigm in the political context of Hong Kong}

        Nevertheless, the political context in Hong Kong does not resemble the ordinary pattern in an authoritarian regime. Whilst the CE and his or her SAR government is accountable 
        to the CPG according to the Basic Law, the relationship between the SAR government and CPG is not strictly one of the lower and higher-level governments in an authoritarian regime because of the high autonomy 
        enjoyed by Hong Kong under the framework of ``One Country, Two Systems'' (`OCTS'). For a long time after the 1997 Handover, in order to maintain a high level of stability and 
        continuity in Hong Kong, not causing panics among the businessmen and ordinary residents in Hong Kong, the CPG took the initiative to refrain from interfering with Hong Kong's 
        political affairs \parencite{so2011onecountry}, and gave Hong Kong a very large room of autonomy even in the area of the CPG's prerogatives, such as diplomacy. As regards the 
        election of CE, since it concerns the ``sovereignty, security and developmental interest'' of the PRC \parencite[18]{chen2017law}, the CPG has to exert a certain level of influence 
        to make sure that the CE-elect was not confrontational to the CPG. But before 2020, such an influence, albeit considerable, was indirect and limited. Beijing had to rely largely 
        on its political coalition with the local business elites to secure a favorable result in the CE election, and a non-China-preferred candidate probably would have won the 2017 
        CE election had this political coalition been shaken  \parencite{kwong2025electoral}. It is thus far-fetched to say that at that time, a CE was hand-picked by the CPG. Hence, 
        it seems that in the pre-2020 Hong Kong, so long as a CE or CE-elect fully respects the CPG's authority and has demonstrated the level of allegiance to the CPG as mandated by 
        the Basic Law, they had little incentive to display additional loyalty through frequent rhetorical references to the central authorities and their political priorities, as such 
        a ``sycophantic act'' was likely to backfire, undermining the CE's support among ordinary Hong Kong residents and the pan-democrats, who constitute a highly influential 
        political force at that time \parencite{ng2020recontextualisation}.

        But things have changed drastically since 2019. The unprecedentedly large-scale anti-extradition law amendment riots had eroded the CPG's faith in Hong Kong to protect the 
        country's ``sovereignty, security and developmental interest'' from the pro-independence and pro-separatism groups on its own \parencite{hung2020unrest}. The landslide victory 
        by pan-democrats and anti-China groups in the 2019 District Council Election has further deepened the CPG's worry about the local regime being `taken over' by separatist forces 
        in the future elections \parencite{hung2020unrest}. Consequently, in 2020, Beijing enacted the NSL and promulgated a decision to disqualify some dissenting legislators, followed 
        by a drastic amendment of the methods for electing the Chief Executive (Annex I to the Basic Law) and the Legislative Council (Annex II to the Basic Law) in 2021, which has not 
        only effectively excluded the opposition from accessing Hong Kong's political system but also reconstituted the pro-Beijing coalition—elevating a new tier of loyalist elites 
        while diminishing the proportion of direct election and marginalizing the traditional capitalist class \parencite{kwong2025electoral}. In this way, Beijing has successfully 
        dominated Hong Kong's executive and legislative systems, becoming the single most important support base for any candidate who wants to run for the CE election and any CE who 
        wants to have effective governance after assuming the office.

        With all these changes, now the political context in Hong Kong seems to match the superior-subordinate pattern for the loyalty-signaling theoretical framework as mentioned above. 
        In light of the theoretical framework, the CEs shall have a high incentive to signal their loyalty to Beijing after 2020, as the social unrest in 2019 has weakened Beijing's 
        trust in Hong Kong's politicians and now a CE's political career and survival depend almost solely upon Beijing's endorsement. This theoretical analysis fits well with the 
        phenomenon observed by many people \parencite{li2022chinese, chan2023ccp}. At the second-half stage of Carrie Lam's administration, her conversation and public pronouncements 
        seemed to mirror Beijing-favored language more frequently, ranging from the reference to political and cultural norms put forward by Beijing, the adoption of Beijing's assertive 
        tone in public addresses to the systematic rebranding of Hong Kong as a subordinate city-region fully integrated into the national development blueprint \parencite{peck2024hongkong}. 
        Such a loyalty-signaling discursive practice has been much more normalized since John Lee assumed office in mid-2022. \textcite{deng2025legitimation} point out that citations 
        of Beijing's official documents and sycophantic declarations of gratitude to Beijing have become standard practices of the incumbent HKSAR government.

    \subsection{Research Focus}

        Though existing scholarly literature has touched upon the Hong Kong SAR government's loyalty-signaling discursive practices \parencite{deng2025legitimation, peck2024hongkong, kwan2023introduction}, 
        their analyses are far from comprehensive and systemic. Some of them only sample the annual Policy Addresses for analysis, yet it overlooks significant loyalty-signaling 
        rhetoric in other public settings, such as the CE's beginning of his Tai Po Fire media briefings by thanking President Xi for his care and support 
        \parencite{govhk2025fire}, and the CE's praise of President Xi at the launch of the President's official publications \parencite{govhk2026speech}.  Other exisiting literature focus 
        predominantly on a concrete policy aspect \parencite{kwan2023introduction}, which has certainly lost sight of other important dimensions. Also, while measuring the density 
        of keywords with loyalty-signaling features in their quantitative analysis, the existing research fails to provide a comprehensive and systematic list of those keywords, 
        merely arbitrarily selecting them without a substantive explanation on how the inclusion of these keywords in a public speech can signal loyalty to Beijing \parencite{deng2025legitimation, peck2024hongkong}. 

        With the above-mentioned research gaps in mind, our research seeks to systematize the analysis of the CEs' loyalty-signaling discourse mainly by i) compiling and rationalizing 
        a comprehensive and categorized list of keywords used by the CEs to display loyalty to Beijing; ii) sampling a larger and more representative scope of public speeches by the CEs, 
        with the press releases delivered by their offices and the central leaders' Hong Kong-related speeches as supplementary reference data. The methodology for executing these 
        research tasks will be elaborated in detail in the next section.
    
\section{Methodology}

    \subsection{Data Sampling}

        The primary dataset consists of 6,994 official press releases and 2,697 speeches and articles in Traditional Chinese, all of which were retrieved from the official websites 
        of the successive CEs. Since these websites serve as a main channel for a CE to communicate with the public and include every significant public address, the data is highly 
        representative of a CE's official voice throughout his or her entire career in office. This corpus spans five administrative terms from July 1997 to March 2026, covering the 
        leadership of Tung Chee-hwa, Donald Tsang, Leung Chun-ying, Carrie Lam, and John Lee.\footnote{Press release for current administration (Traditional Chinese) can be found at: 
        \url{https://www.ceo.gov.hk/tc/press.html}; CE speeches: \url{https://www.ceo.gov.hk/tc/speeches.html}; Archive of past CE's speeches and press releases:
        \url{https://www.ceo.gov.hk/tc/links.html}}
        
        By measuring the density of the Loyalty Signaling Keywords in the corpus, we may find out how the loyalty-signaling discursive practices vary among the five CEs. To facilitate 
        a comparative analysis, addresses by central leaders regarding Hong Kong were collected as a proxy for Mainland political discourse. By evaluating the linguistic similarity 
        between these two corpora, this paper can the degree of rhetorical alignment and discursive convergence between Hong Kong and the CPG. 
               
        
    \subsection{Conceptualizing ``Loyalty Signaling''}

        According to the theoretical framework laid out at the beginning, public discourse is an important channel for lower-level officials to display loyalty to their superiors 
        in an authoritarian regime \parencite{baturo2025personalization}, but what to include in the public speeches as loyalty signals might vary in different regime contexts.

        Within the sphere of Chinese politics, the concept of `loyalty' is multifaceted and nuanced. While traditional political culture often emphasizes personal allegiance to 
        immediate superiors, the official discourse explicitly prioritizes `loyalty to the Party'—a term that remains strategically ambiguous. Does that translate into Hong Kong 
        political discourse as well? How do CEs signal loyalty to the CPG?

        This section aims to justify our current categorization of keywords into six types. It is not only a thematic grouping, but a structural mapping based on 
        loyalty-signaling theory. As established by \textcite{huang2015propaganda}, official propaganda in an authoritarian context often functions not to persuade, but to signal 
        an agent's submission. The six categories below capture how the Hong Kong government utilizes specific linguistic markers to signal alignment with the central authorities, 
        in order to bridge the ``political trust deficit'' with Beijing.

        To ensure the analysis is comprehensive, these keywords are divided into six categories, conceptually grouped into three layers: Authority (Who), Strategy (What), and 
        Cognition (How).

        \subsubsection{Authority signals}
            
            This layer identifies the source of political mandate and confirms the hierarchy of obedience. This is captured through two primary dimensions: personal loyalty and 
            institutional loyalty.

            \textit{i. Personal loyalty}

            The inclusion of keywords related to the top leadership (such as their names and positions) is designed to measure the transition from ``rule-based'' to ``leader-based'' 
            legitimacy. In an appointive political hierarchy, such rhetoric serves as ``political credentialing.'' As suggested by \textcite{baturo2025playing}, reference to the name and political 
            agenda of the top leader is an effective way to signal loyalty in an authoritarian regime. By grounding local initiatives in the ``Spirit'' or instructions of the ``Core'' 
            leader, the CE used name projections to signal that the administration's legitimacy is derived from supreme authority rather than local bureaucratic tradition.

            \textit{ii. Institutional loyalty}

            This category tracks keywords related to central party institutions and state organs (e.g., the Central Office of Hong Kong and Macao Affairs, 20th Party Congress). By consistently referencing central institutions, 
            the CE symbolically validates the CPG's oversight and ``plenary power to govern Hong Kong'', proactively acknowledging the principal's presence and authority. 
            
        \subsubsection{Strategic alignment}

            This layer measures the redirection of policy objectives and the reconstruction of Hong Kong's identity.

            \textit{iii. Policy Loyalty}

            As pointed out by \textcite{peck2024hongkong}, in the wake of the 2019 riot and the NSL in 2020, the CE has changed her discursive emphasis from the unique strength of 
            Hong Kong as an open metropolis and the classical liberal model it relied on to thrive in the past decades, to Hong Kong's instrumental role in national development and 
            the imperative need of Hong Kong to integrate into the nation's policy blueprint. Even in the area of climate policy, the SAR government has frequently adopted Mainland 
            government's framing style in its policy documents since 2019 \parencite{kwan2023introduction}. Such changes in terms of the policy alignment are undoubtedly an important 
            discursive practice to express Hong Kong's subordination to Beijing. In order to capture this discursive paradigm, we set up this category which focuses on keywords 
            that align local governance with national planning (e.g., Great Bay Area, 14th Five-Year Plan). By transforming local administrative tasks into national strategic 
            contributions, Hong Kong government uses performance-based signals to prove they are executing Central Authority's will. By framing ``Hong Kong's strengths'' as instruments 
            for ``the country's needs,'' the CE demonstrates functional utility, proving that the SAR is a productive asset in the national development strategy, thereby signaling loyalty to Beijing.

            \textit{iv. Nationalistic Loyalty}

            As noted by \textcite{kwan2023introduction}, Hong Kong SAR government has repeatedly highlighted its relationship and proximity with Mainland China since 2019, which is 
            also an important category of discourse for signaling loyalty. Keywords like ``Integration'' [融合] and ``Backing the Motherland'' [背靠祖国] go beyond simple patriotism, 
            and signify a relational re-anchoring, where Hong Kong is re-defined as a functional dependency of the national whole. By establishing a ``mother-child'' or ``supply-demand'' 
            narrative, the Hong Kong government actively signals that Hong Kong's identity is now inextricably tied to its proximity and contribution to the motherland.


        \subsubsection{Cognitive convergence}

            This layer captures the deepest level of alignment, the adoption of the principal's worldview and linguistic habits.

            \textit{v. Ideological loyalty}

            This category includes high-level political concepts (e.g., ``Chinese-style Modernization'' [中國式現代化], ``Struggle Spirit'' [鬥爭精神]). The rationale is to determine the 
            extent to which the Hong Kong bureaucracy is adopting the party state's epistemological framework. This signals that the Hong Kong government is not merely following orders but is 
            actively learning to ``think'' in synchronization with Beijing.


            \textit{vi. Rhetorical loyalty}

            This dimension captures the core of ``officialese'', involving mainland-style vocabularies such as ``Unswervingly'' [堅定不移], ``To the letter'' [不折不扣], ``High-level design'' 
            [高屋建瓴]. This represents a form of ``Mimetic Isomorphism'' \parencite{dimaggio1983iron}, where an organization, facing political uncertainty, adopts the precise communicative 
            rituals of the dominant power to gain legitimacy. By mimicking the rhetorical tempo of the CPG, the CE demonstrates that his or her administration is ``fully disciplined,'' 
            using symbolic compliance to secure political safety and minimizing the risk of interpretive variance.

    \subsection{Keyword Detection}

		The data cleaning phase involved adjusting character encoding for the accurate rendering of Traditional Chinese characters, handling missing values, and stripping HTML tags, punctuation, and extraneous whitespace. A length threshold was enforced, excluding any documents with fewer than 2,000 characters. Additionally, temporal metadata was standardized by extracting years of publication via regular expressions and formatting dates to DD-MM-YYYY, restricting the analytical window strictly to the 1997–2026 period.
        
        Data wrangling procedures include transforming the unstructured corpus into structured DataFrames for quantitative analysis, and conducting text normalization and stop word removal to prepare the text for subsequent processing.

        The following computational steps are performed to transform raw text into measurable density metrics:
        
        \begin{itemize}
            \item Temporal categorization: a regular expression function, \inlinecode{extract_year}, was used to identify the year of publication from the file names.
            \item Keyword extraction: for every document, the script iterated through the dictionary and counted the total occurrences (Hits) of every defined keyword.
            \item Normalization and density metrics: to account for varying document lengths, the \textit{Loyalty Signaling Keyword Density} was calculated using the following formula:
            
            \begin{equation}
                \text{Density} = \left( \frac{\sum \text{Keyword Hits}}{\text{Total Character Count}} \right) \times 10,000
            \end{equation}
            
            This normalization yields a standardized metric representing keyword frequency per 10,000 characters, allowing for a statistically valid longitudinal comparison across the dataset.
            
        \end{itemize}


	\subsection{Cosine Similarity Analysis}

        The CEs' speeches and Hong Kong-related discourse from the central leaders are parsed using regular expressions to extract publication dates and categorized into annual corpora. 
        The raw text undergoes rigorous cleaning, including the removal of non-Chinese characters and HTML tags. The \inlinecode{jieba} library is used for precise Chinese semantic segmentation.

        To isolate ``loyalty signaling' from routine administrative language, loyalty keyword boosting and customized stop-word filter are implemented. The TF-IDF algorithm is implemented to 
        transform unstructured text into high-dimensional numerical vectors. The model utilizes a \inlinecode{max_df} of 0.85 and a \inlinecode{min_df} of 2 to remove extremely common or statistically insignificant tokens.

        The degree of discursive convergence is quantified using Cosine Similarity.
        \begin{equation}
            \text{Cosine Similarity} = \frac{\mathbf{A} \cdot \mathbf{B}}{\|\mathbf{A}\| \|\mathbf{B}\|}
        \end{equation}
       
        where $\mathbf{A}$ and $\mathbf{B}$ represent the TF-IDF vectors of CE speeches and central leader addresses, respectively. This metric ranges from 0 (completely dissimilar) to 1 (identical), providing a quantitative 
        measure of rhetorical alignment over time.
        

\section{Results and Findings}

    \subsection{Loyalty Signaling in CE Speeches}

        The density of loyalty-signaling keywords in CEs' speeches varied significantly across administrations (ANOVA result: F(4,2692) = 558.02, p < .001). Tung established a moderate 
        baseline for loyalty-signaling rhetoric, which remained relatively stable throughout his tenure, marked by a slight uptick preceding his resignation. Following Tung, loyalty signaling plummeted. For 
        both Tsang and Leung, the median keyword density dropped to zero, indicating that in at least half of their analyzed speeches, loyalty-signaling keywords did not appear a single time. Although Figure \ref{fig:keyword_density}
        shows a visual upward trend in signaling at the end of Leung's term, statistical analysis demonstrates no significant difference in the overall signaling density between Tsang and Leung (See Table \ref{tab:ce_stats}).
            
        A dramatic escalation in loyalty signaling occurred during Carrie Lam's administration. The frequency climbed steadily from 2017, reaching its peak in 2020. Her mean loyalty-signaling density was 
        more than double of Tung's and nearly five times higher than Leung's. This rhetorical shift reached its absolute triumph under John Lee's administration. Not only is his keyword density 
        the highest, his lowest Coefficient of Variation (CV) further suggests that his high volume of loyalty signaling is not confined to specific events; it is a standardized, highly consistent, 
        and baked-in component of his everyday executive discourse.

        Chronologically, the year of 2015 serves as a critical inflection point, representing the immediate rhetorical response to the 2014 Umbrella Movement. This shift was preceded by the State 
        Council's June 2014 White Paper, which systemically introduced the doctrine of "Comprehensive Jurisdiction" [全面管治] for the first time.\footnote {See \url{https://www.hmo.gov.cn/xwzx/zwyw/202312/t20231208_38591.html}

        conclusion that loyalty signaling in the HKSAR is most acute during periods of peak political uncertainty and serves as a reparative mechanism to restore central-local confidence.

        While the overall trend for ``loyalty signaling'' shows a statistically significant increase during the tenures of Carrie Lam and John Lee, this pattern is not uniform across all categories (as suggested by Figure \ref{fig:category_trend}). First of 
        all, there is no rhetorical loyalty signaling throughout all the periods. Secondly, ideological loyalty has constantly remained the most dominant category in terms of absolute volume, reaching a density of 
        120.4 per 10,000 characters in Lee's administration. But it shall be noted that Tung Chee-hwa also exhibited a high propensity for ideological signaling; as the first Chief Executive, he had to frequently 
        articulate the relationship between the HKSAR and the mainland socialist system. Thirdly, none of the five CEs demonstrated a significant enthusiasm for ``personal loyalty'' signaling, indicating that 
        political alignment in the HKSAR is projected as an institutional or systemic commitment rather than an individual allegiance.

        what they said and what their administrations published. When overall loyalty signaling was low, it was uniformly low across both channels.

        being signaled through official press releases than through the Chief Executive's personal speeches. While it remains unclear whether this marks an outlier or a strategic realignment in public discourse, 
        two hypotheses offer potential explanations for this phenomenon. The first hypothesis is that, having spent 2022 to 2025 establishing an undeniably high baseline of political alignment, the administration 
        may now consider that trust secured. The burden of loyalty signaling is being pushed down into the civil service machinery through the standardized, boilerplate text of daily press releases. Another 
        hypothesis is that the administration, cautious of potential backlash from civil society and the commercial sector—where repeated loyalty signaling might shake confidence in Hong Kong's autonomy—the 
        administration may be intentionally shifting these signals into less visible bureaucratic channels. This allows the Chief Executive to reserve his or her highly visible public speeches for pragmatic, economically oriented messaging.
    
    \subsection{Textual Similarity}

        The consistently low overall textual similarity between Chief Executive speeches and state leaders' Hong Kong-related addresses highlights a crucial finding: loyalty signaling has not driven a wholesale assimilation into Mainland political rhetoric (See Figure \ref{fig:similarity_trend} in Appendix B). This demonstrates that the recent rise in loyalty signaling has not fundamentally changed the underlying structure of Hong Kong's political discourse. Despite a higher frequency of specific political keywords, there is no empirical evidence to suggest that the CE has adopted the generalized ``officialese'' often characteristic of Mainland bureaucratic texts.
        
        Notably, contrary to the hypothesis that John Lee might heavily utilize Mainland-style bureaucratic jargon to approximate Beijing's political discourse, it appears form the data that he employs a highly targeted, ``modular'' signaling strategy. Rather than structurally adopting Mainland-style rhetorical style, Lee strategically inserts specific loyalty-signaling keywords (such as ``national security'' and ``patriots administering Hong Kong'') with high frequency and precision to establish an unambiguous political baseline. However, his overarching narrative style, daily administrative vocabulary, and linguistic structure have not converged with the Mainland's official speech system. This represents a highly pragmatic rhetorical approach: it guarantees strict political compliance while actively preserving the localized, pragmatic, and technocratic linguistic framework inherent to Hong Kong's executive branch.

        
       

\section{Conclusion and Discussion}
    
    This paper has elucidated the dynamic evolution of loyalty-signaling discursive practices by Hong Kong's Chief Executives from 1997 to early 2026, revealing that political 
    alignment is a strategic tool rather than a constant or permanent posture. The findings demonstrate that loyalty signaling serves as a reparative mechanism, intensifying 
    significantly following periods of socio-political friction, such as the 2014 Umbrella Movement and the 2019 Anti-Extradition Law Amendment Movement, to regain political 
    confidence from the Central Government. While the frequency of such signals remained moderate or low during the terms of Tung Chee-hwa, Donald Tsang, and Leung Chun-ying, 
    it experienced a dramatic surge under Carrie Lam and reached a peak of standardized consistency during John Lee's administration. However, the data refutes the hypothesis 
    of a wholesale assimilation, namely fully adopting the mainland rhetorical styles; instead, it reveals a ``modular'' strategy where specific political baselines are established 
    through high-precision keywords while the underlying technocratic and pragmatic features of HKSAR government's official discourse remain preserved. The observed shift in 
    2026, where signaling density in official press releases surpassed that of personal speeches, further suggests a transition towards the institutionalization of loyalty 
    within the bureaucratic apparatus. In this more invisible manner, the CE and his administration may signal loyalty to Beijing without undermining the faith of businessmen 
    and residents in the ``high autonomy'' of Hong Kong under the OCTS framework. With the full picture of results in mind, it seems that the political elites in Hong Kong, 
    represented by the CE, generally do not signal loyalty to Beijing for its own sake; rather, they skilfully express loyalty with the aim of reassuring Beijing that the 
    ``Two Systems'' element would not undermine the ``One Country'' component under the OCTS framework, so that they can gradually regain more political room for the SAR. 
    When Beijing rests fully assured of the loyalty of Hong Kong's political elites, the democratization process as outlined and envisaged in the Basic Law might go back on 
    the political agenda.

    However, several limitations of this research must be acknowledged. First, the analysis relies exclusively on public speeches and press releases, which may represent a 
    ``front-stage'' performance that does not fully capture the complexities of private, ``back-stage'' interactions between SAR officials and Beijing. Second, since we used 
    keyword detection and text similarity as the major methodologies, despite the fact that the quantitative metrics provide a robust macro-level overview, they may overlook 
    the subtle linguistic nuances, tone, or coded metaphors that characterize political communication beyond specific keyword densities. Finally, the focus on Chinese-language 
    documents leaves a gap in understanding how the CEs manage multi-audience signaling, particularly when addressing international stakeholders in English.

    Despite these constraints, this research contributes to the existing literature by enriching the academic understanding of the loyalty-signaling discursive paradigm in 
    semi-autonomous contexts and systematizing the analysis of loyalty-displaying discourses in Hong Kong. By developing a six-dimensional keyword framework to concretize 
    ``loyalty,'' this study moves beyond narrating qualitative anecdotes to providing a measurable and empirical basis for analyzing political alignment. Methodologically, 
    the application of text mining and semantic similarity analysis offers a replicable model for studying political discourse in other Chinese provinces or autonomous regions. 
    Looking forward, this research provides a foundation for future inquiries into the audience reception of modular signaling, particularly among the business sector and 
    international community, regarding whether there are significant audience costs of such high-density loyalty rhetoric. Future studies could also expand this framework to 
    investigate whether the current linguistic balance is a sustainable equilibrium or a transitional phase that will eventually converge with mainland political rhetoric.
